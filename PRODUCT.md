# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Researchers running systematic reviews and meta-analyses: clinicians, graduate students, and
information specialists. They arrive with a draft PubMed strategy, often on a laptop between
other work and sometimes on a phone, and want to test it quickly: how many records it
retrieves, and whether the studies they already know about are found.

## Product Purpose

ssHelper is a free public web app for building and testing systematic review search
strategies. The page opens with a short landing block that says what the tool does and who
made it, then goes straight into the tool: strategy arms, PubMed counts, a known-studies check,
and search history. Success means a researcher understands the tool in seconds and reaches the
strategy editor without scrolling past marketing, including on a smartphone.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, calm, personal. Academic confidence without academic stiffness: precise wording,
quiet visuals, and a clear human author (Vanio Antunes, meta-analysis researcher) standing
behind the tool. Keep the current restrained look; add personality through the author's
presence and careful detail, not through decoration.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: big gradient heroes, stock illustrations, fake metrics,
  "Get started free" clutter, identical feature card grids.
- Dated academic or university-portal sites: cramped tiny text, grey tables, old styling.

## Design Principles

1. The tool is the product. The landing block stays compact so the editor is visible early,
   even at 390px wide.
2. Show, don't sell. State plainly what the tool does; let the working tool prove it.
3. Researcher to researcher. A real person made this; the author presence builds trust.
4. Precision over flourish. Every word, count, and status must be exact and honest.
5. Calm under load. Long strategies and many studies must stay readable, never busy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA, enforced by axe checks in the end-to-end tests: color contrast, 24px minimum
target size, full keyboard use with visible focus, and screen-reader names for every control.
Status is never conveyed by color alone (icon plus text). Respect reduced-motion preferences.
